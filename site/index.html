The idea

Culture is the asset. Creators own the economics.

Prediction products are built for traders, so they leave out the culture and creators that drive attention. MAD Gambit turns that attention into markets with fair economics for the people who create it.

01 · PROBLEM

Built for traders

Binary contracts reward people who already trade. The viral moment, the creator who started it and the crowd that cares are all outside the market.

02 · SOLUTION

Attention becomes a market

A creator turns a live headline into a market. People take a position through a card or a stake. The market prices continuously and does not wait for an outcome to settle.

03 · WEDGE

Entertainment and creator culture

Every viral headline or creator moment becomes a market people can back with conviction — specific ground a generic binary venue does not own.

Binary vs continuous

A binary market asks one question and closes. A conviction market is priced every moment, so a storyline with no clean ending is still tradable.

Five things that don't exist elsewhere

  • The market never has to close. No event-fatigue cliff, because there is no event.
  • The creator is the counterparty. A traction-gated profit-share ladder, not an affiliate fee.
  • A market whose subject is a belief. Perpetual Epiphanies prices mental models and adages; they resolve never, by construction.
  • Loss mitigation priced off the book. MAD Shield is opt-in, fee-funded, capped — never insurance.
  • Deterministic classification. No machine learning in the taxonomy: the first matching rule wins, so every split is a sentence a user can read.

Each is a design claim with a state — see "Where the build stands" below.

The live maths engine

Six steps from headline to price.

Every claim carries a state: Implemented in code, In design, Planned. The code is the implementation authority. Equations are shown in general form; coefficients and weights stay server-side.

Signal

Implemented

News and the open web are scored for scandal velocity from GDELT matches. Emotion is weighted, and a happy story is a bad perpetual: markets are opened by a signal, not by hand.

p(perp) = 1 − exp[ −k · n · w · (1 + v) ]
n = matches · w = theme weight · v = scandal velocity

Above the gate a storyline opens as a perpetual rather than a binary. The curve saturates: roughly 0.87 at eight matches and 0.95 at twelve.

Classify

Implemented

A deterministic priority cascade decides what kind of market a storyline is. The first rule that matches wins, which is why a long gossip perpetual can never be filed as a saga, and why the classifier also writes the market title and resolution criteria.

first_match( rule₁, rule₂, … ) → market type

No machine learning in the taxonomy. Legible odds need legible rules.

Conviction

Implemented

How much a position means, not how large it is. A Cobb-Douglas score composes tenure, capital, depth, psychology and odds, with amplifiers for meme, satire, liar and volatility.

M = Π xᵢ^wᵢ × amplifiers

Exponents and weights are proprietary and shared under NDA in the data room.

Volatility

Implemented

GARCH(1,1) lets yesterday's turbulence inform today's. A clamp keeps a market interesting without turning it into a casino: conviction is amplified, never detonated.

σ²ₜ = ω + α·ε²ₜ₋₁ + β·σ²ₜ₋₁  ·  σ clamped to [0.09, 0.68]

The floor sits above the unconditional level, so the clamp binds even at rest.

Price

Implemented

A continuous book with a dynamic-liquidity LMSR. Buying moves the price along a curve instead of matching an order, and the liquidity parameter scales with volume.

C(q) = b · ln Σ e^(qᵢ/b)  ·  pᵢ = softmax(q/b)  ·  cost = C(q + δeᵢ) − C(q)

What the liquidity parameter should be at low volume is not yet modelled — an open item, not a hidden one.

Protect

Implemented

MAD Shield is opt-in, fee-funded loss mitigation with capped recovery. Its premium is priced off the same book as the bet, so protection costs more where the market is more volatile. It is not insurance and it is not a guarantee.

premium ∝ stake × P(win) × σ × K  ·  clamped to 2–4% of stake
recovery: 28.8% standard, rising to a 40% maximum

Shield solvency modelling is not yet published.
