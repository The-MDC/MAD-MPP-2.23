Disclosure rule — the Z-Liar Meter prices a statement, never a speaker. Policy A market surface carries the claim and only the claim. Even where the speaker is known with certainty, the platform does not name them — no proper noun in the title, no handle, no photograph, no logo, no role descriptor specific enough to identify one person.

This is not a hedge bolted on afterwards. The code already says it: Z-Liar is "the only family whose subject is a claim rather than a storyline." The disclosure rule is that architectural fact enforced at the surface, and it is what makes a continuously-running truth meter defensible rather than a standing publication about a person.

It is absolute, not surface-deep. The platform does not name the speaker anywhere — not in the market, not in the resolution record, not in the settlement event. The subject of a Z-Liar market is a statement, and that is the only thing the system stores about it.

What that costs, and how it is paid: resolution still requires independent corroboration, and a corroborating source will usually name the speaker. So the record stores a pointer, not a person — the source URL and a content hash of the statement as published. A resolver can verify the claim against the source; the chain never holds the identity. Corroboration without attribution.
Two consequences that must be handled, not assumed away.
(1) De-identification is harder than omitting a name. A verbatim quote plus a date plus a context routinely identifies a person as effectively as naming them — and under an absolute rule that applies to the stored record too, not only the display. The rule needs a concrete test: would a reader who follows the news identify the speaker from what the system stores? If yes, the market does not open. That test belongs in market-classifier.ts, not in a style guide. It is also the single cheapest thing to build on this list.
(2) Gossip-Cop is now the exposed surface, not Z-Liar. Gossip-Cop is defined as celebrity and tabloid sagas with roast titles and satirical framing — inherently about named people. The statement-only rule does not reach it. Whatever protects Z-Liar leaves Gossip-Cop as the family carrying the real publication risk, and it needs its own policy before launch. Unresolved
Open-Market Perpetual — three questions to close before it goes in front of anyone. (1) What is the gate — the boolean or threshold that routes a market here rather than to Gossip-Cop or Epiphanies? (2) What is the leg length and the roll condition? (3) Does it need its own enum value, or does it file under perp the way Epiphanies does? Epiphanies earned no enum because the gossip boolean was enough to route it; Z-Liar and Gossip-Cop each earned one because their resolution semantics differ. Same test applies here.

Contract architecture — built on the Morpho pattern

The contracts follow Morpho Blue's design discipline: a minimal immutable core, permissionless market creation, and risk isolated per market rather than pooled across the protocol. A Morpho market is defined by five fixed parameters — collateral asset, loan asset, LLTV, oracle, interest-rate model — and once deployed, the rules never change. Morpho docs, verified

Why copy it
Isolation beats pooling

A bad market cannot drain a good one. For a platform whose whole premise is permissionless creator-originated markets, pooled risk would be fatal — one manipulated gossip perpetual would reach every position on the venue.

Immutable core
Rules that cannot be changed

Matches the canon already asserted: the rake and the creator ladder are constants, never on the governance board. Morpho's pattern is that promise enforced by deployment, not by policy.

Auditability
A small core is a small audit

The security line item in the bridge is far cheaper against a minimal immutable core than a large upgradeable one. This choice reduces the cost of the thing that has not been funded yet.

State this as a pattern, not a partnership. Following Morpho's architecture and integrating Morpho for micro-loans are two different claims. The integration is planned, not built — no deployed contracts, no agreement. Say "built on the Morpho pattern, with Morpho integration planned for lending" and nothing stronger.
